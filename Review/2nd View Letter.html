6. Review Comments to the Author

Reviewer #2: The authors have responded appropriately to my comments and explain both how they addressed them and how they highlighted them in the relevant section of the article.

I have only three important points left to address.

1) In the current Tables 8 and 9, all values related to "Hate Speech Detection" and "Fake News Detection" (for all models) have not changed from the original version. It appears that these values still correspond to the F1 scores, but not to the newly calculated Macro-F1 scores.

2) The figures showing the class-wise performance analysis (Figs. 11, 12, 13, 14) present F1-score percentages, rather than Macro-F1 scores, as shown in Tables 8 and 9.

3) The sections titled "LLMs performance across sentiment categories" and "LLMs performance across emotion categories" contain a lot of repeated text (copied and pasted). They need to be reviewed and corrected.

Reviewer #3: The paper evaluates six open-source LLMs across four Bengali classification tasks using zero- and five-shot prompting. My recommendation: Major Revision - but the paper is potentially publishable after a focused methodological/statistical revision. Must fix: The paper cannot really establish that “model size” causes better performance. The claim “larger models constantly surpass smaller ones across all tasks” is too strong. Statistical analysis is improved. The study evaluates six models, but inferential testing focuses only on the two strongest models. Use a paired multi-model analysis across the six models, followed by corrected pairwise comparisons. Multiple-testing correction is missing. One fixed set of five demonstrations is not enough to robustly measure the effect of few-shot prompting. There is a conceptual problem with the claim that few-shot prompting improves Bengali understanding - I recommend change to “improve classification performance under the evaluated Bengali task settings.”. The paper should distinguish “benchmark performance” from “real-world effectiveness”. Error analysis is much better, but still needs quantitative depth. The manuscript contains some terminology that should be cleaned up. The model naming needs absolute consistency. One particularly important issue: “model size” and “multilingual pretraining” are being conflated. In summary, next revision primarily on scientific positioning and statistical rigor rather than adding many more experiments:

1. Remove/soften causal claims about model size.
2. Remove “always/constantly” language.
3. Clarify that only Qwen vs Gemma were statistically compared.
4. Address multiple-comparison correction.
5. Qualify the five-shot result because only one demonstration set/seed was used.
6. Clarify that fake-news downsampling changes the evaluation distribution.
7. Replace “understanding” claims with “classification performance” claims where appropriate.
8. Ensure exact model names/revisions are consistent throughout.
9. Strengthen the limitations around model-family confounding.
10. Add quantitative error-category analysis.
11. Discuss English versus Bengali prompting as a limitation.
12. Consider “open-weight” rather than “open-source.”
13. Report exact software/model/tokenizer versions.
14. Make the Discussion substantially more analytical and less descriptive.

7. PLOS authors have the option to publish the peer review history of their article (what does this mean?). If published, this will include your full peer review and any attached files.


If you choose “no”, your identity will remain anonymous but your review may still be made public.


Do you want your identity to be public for this peer review? For information about this choice, including consent withdrawal, please see our Privacy Policy.

Reviewer #2: No

Reviewer #3: Yes: Dr. Biraja Ghoshal
